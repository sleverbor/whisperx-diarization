# YouTube captions on MossFormer2 priority clips

The automatic English caption track from the exact source video was aligned to the 15 human-reviewed overlap intervals. The full caption track is not retained in this evaluation; its hash records the source used.

Among the six clips labeled useful, captions supported the existing baseline more strongly in four and gave similar support in two. Captions did not favor the separated candidate in any useful clip. When captions favored separated wording, the human judgment said the clip was wrong, garbled, or the wrong speaker. This means the candidate may contain real words while still being unsuitable as target speech.

Captions are useful as independent timing and wording evidence. They do not contain reliable speaker identity and cannot authorize insertion into the target transcript. The appropriate product behavior is to show caption wording beside uncertain audio during review while preserving its provenance.
